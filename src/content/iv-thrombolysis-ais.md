IV thrombolysis in acute ischaemic stroke

Initial assessment and treatment windows

A clearly disabling deficit can qualify for intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) even when the NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score is low. Exclude acute intracranial haemorrhage with noncontrast CT or MRI before treatment. 2026 AHA/ASA guideline, sections 4.6.1–4.6.3

|Presentation |When to consider IVT |
|---|---|
|**Within 4.5 hours of onset or last known well** |Treat eligible patients as quickly as possible. |
|**Wake-up or unknown-onset stroke, MRI pathway** |Consider IVT within 4.5 hours of symptom recognition if the diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) lesion occupies less than one-third of the middle cerebral artery territory and has no marked corresponding FLAIR signal change. **Class 2a.**|
|**4.5–9 hours from last known well, or wake-up stroke within 9 hours of the midpoint of sleep**|Consider IVT in selected patients who are ineligible for endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) and have salvageable tissue on automated CT or MR perfusion imaging. **Class 2a.** |
|**4.5–24 hours from onset or last known well** |In selected patients with a large-vessel occlusion (LVO), salvageable tissue, and no option for EVT, a stroke specialist may consider IVT. **Class 2b.** |

Contraindications highlighted in the slides

Absolute contraindications

• Imaging. Acute intracranial haemorrhage or extensive, clearly established CT hypodensity that corresponds to the presenting deficit.
• Recent injury or surgery. Moderate-to-severe traumatic brain injury within 14 days with qualifying clinical or imaging findings, intracranial or spinal surgery within 14 days, or spinal cord injury within 3 months.
• Severe coagulopathy or thrombocytopenia. Platelets <100,000/µL, INR >1.7, aPTT >40 seconds, or PT >15 seconds.
• Other high-risk conditions. Intra-axial intracranial tumour, suspected infective endocarditis, known or suspected aortic arch dissection, amyloid-related imaging abnormalities (ARIA), or current anti-amyloid immunotherapy.

Relative contraindications

• Pre-existing disability or frailty. Weigh the chance of returning to baseline against treatment risk.
• Ischaemic stroke within 3 months. Consider the timing and size of the previous infarct.
• Prior intracerebral haemorrhage. Consider its cause. Suspected cerebral amyloid angiopathy raises greater concern than a corrected cause such as coagulopathy.
• Direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) intake within the previous 48 hours. Use the individual assessment below.

IV thrombolysis after recent DOAC intake

For an otherwise eligible patient with a disabling ischaemic stroke within the alteplase or tenecteplase treatment window, the guideline allows consideration of IVT after an individual benefit–risk assessment. Safety after DOAC intake in the previous 48 hours remains uncertain. Consider the specific drug, time of the last dose, renal function, stroke severity, and the availability of EVT, rapid drug-specific assays and reversal agents. Weigh test turnaround, any delay to IVT and the potential thrombotic risk of reversal. 2026 AHA/ASA guideline, Table 8

In Meinel et al.’s retrospective multicentre study, symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage occurred in 2.5% of 832 IVT-treated patients with recent DOAC intake and 4.1% of IVT-treated controls without anticoagulant use (adjusted OR 0.57, 95% CI 0.36–0.92). Selection for treatment limits generalisability. Definitive trials are needed to establish safety. Meinel et al., JAMA Neurology, 2023

Assess residual anticoagulant activity

Normal PT/INR or aPTT, including a routine point-of-care INR, cannot reliably exclude residual DOAC activity. ICSH laboratory guidance · ACC laboratory testing guidance

|DOAC |Appropriate assay |
|---|---|
|**Dabigatran** |Standard thrombin time (TT) is a sensitive qualitative screen. For quantification, use dilute TT, an ecarin-based assay, or a dabigatran-calibrated anti-IIa assay.|
|**Apixaban, rivaroxaban or edoxaban**|Use a chromogenic anti-factor Xa assay calibrated for the specific drug. |
|**Any DOAC** |Liquid chromatography–tandem mass spectrometry (LC–MS/MS) is the reference method for measuring plasma drug concentration. |

Reperfusion decision and documentation

• LVO eligible for thrombectomy. Activate the EVT pathway immediately and assess IVT eligibility in parallel. Keep EVT moving while the IVT decision is made. 2026 AHA/ASA guideline, sections 4.7.1–4.7.2 and Table 8
• Distal occlusion or perforator stroke. The presenter proposes considering off-label IVT when the deficit is disabling, the patient is within the treatment window, EVT is unsuitable, and the individual bleeding risk is judged low. This is a proposed application of the guideline’s individual assessment. Safety after recent DOAC intake remains uncertain in these subtypes. 2026 AHA/ASA guideline, Table 8
• Document the decision. Record the DOAC and last dose time, renal function, stroke severity, assay results, and any reversal agent used. 2026 AHA/ASA guideline, Table 8
